# Notes should be self-contained

The test for whether a note is the right size: read it cold, six months from now, with none of its
neighbours open. If it still says something, it's a note. If it only makes sense sitting next to
the thing you wrote it beside, it's a fragment, and no amount of linking will rescue it.

This is the failure mode on the opposite side from [[A note with no links is nearly worthless]].
Cut too coarsely and you get an essay nobody can link *to* — there's no single idea to point at.
Cut too finely and you get shards that need a tour guide. One idea, stated in full, is the target.

The practical version: write the claim in the title, and make the body defend it. A title like
"Auth" tells you nothing when it shows up in a link; "Session tokens should outlive access tokens"
tells you what you'll get before you click, which is what makes the link worth following.

Worth pairing with [[Write the note when the thought is still warm]] — most fragments exist
because someone dumped a half-formed thought and never came back to finish it.
